Who We Are

About the Fellowship

Mission

Our mission is to support men in the quest for lives of compassion, integrity, responsibility, and balance.

Through mutually supportive work, our fellowship seeks to help men in their development as sons... Fathers... Brothers... Companions and members of society.

We are a caring, intentionally diverse brotherhood, committed to social justice and offering opportunities for intimate sharing, personal growth, and spiritual deepening.

You don't need to be a member of First UU San Diego, and you don't need to identify with any religion. You just need to be a man who wants deeper community.

Core Values

01
Compassion

For ourselves and those around us — without condition or reserve.

02
Integrity

Showing up honestly in all our relationships, especially the hard ones.

03
Responsibility

To our families, our communities, and the wider world we share.

04
Balance

Nurturing our whole selves — mind, body, and spirit.

How the Fellowship Works

The fellowship operates through three layers of community, each offering a different depth of engagement.

Fourth Mondays

Monthly gathering open to all men. Potluck 6pm, check-in and discussion 7–9pm. Most meetings on Zoom.

Renewal Weekends

Three-day mountain retreats each Fall and Spring. Life-changing events — newcomers especially welcome.

Focus Groups

Small groups of 5–8 men meeting regularly. Built on trust, confidentiality, and long-term commitment.

Steering Committee

Guided by a consensus-model committee. All members welcome to participate in governance.

You don't need to be eloquent or spiritually advanced. Just be willing to be present.

Deep Community

Focus Groups

Focus groups are the heart of long-term community in the fellowship. While the Fourth Monday meetings are the broad circle — welcoming to all, open and varied — focus groups are where the deepest work happens.

Each group meets regularly at times set by the members themselves, at homes or on Zoom. Five to eight men. No agenda beyond showing up honestly.

Many men join UUMF for the monthly meetings and stay for the focus groups. The depth of relationship that develops over months and years of consistent sharing is unlike most friendships men have access to in everyday life.

01
Small & Intimate

Five to eight men. Every voice heard.

02
Confidential

What is shared stays in the group.

03
Long-Term

Groups meet over months and years. Depth comes from continuity.

04
Member-Led

Groups set their own schedule and format. No external agenda.

A Typical Meeting

Opening

A chant, silence, or centering. Something that marks the transition into the group's space.

Check-In

Each man shares what's alive for him. No cross-talk. Just presence and listening.

Deeper Work

One or more men go deeper on something from check-in. The group listens and supports — without fixing, advising, or judging.

Joining a Focus Group

Interest begins at the Fourth Monday meeting. Any Steering Committee member can connect you with the Focus Group Coordinator, who matches men to open groups based on availability and fit.

"I joined the fellowship for the monthly meetings. I stayed because of my focus group. These are the men who know me most — who've been there through the hardest years and the best ones."
— A UUMF member

Connect & Join

There's no application, no dues, no membership requirement. Joining is as simple as showing up to a Fourth Monday gathering.

01
Show Up to a Fourth Monday

Open to all men. Potluck 6pm at the Common Room, Hillcrest Campus, First UU San Diego. Come at 7pm for discussion if you'd rather skip the food your first time.

02
Introduce Yourself

At the start of the meeting there's a check-in round. New faces are always welcomed. Say as much or as little as you'd like.

03
Get on the Mailing List

Ask a co-convener to add you. You'll receive monthly reminders about meeting dates, special events, and renewals.

04
Keep Coming Back

Community builds through consistency. There's no formal membership — belonging is earned through presence.

FAQ

Is this a religious group?
No religious affiliation required. UUMF is rooted in UU principles — inclusive, non-creedal, spiritually open. Men of any faith background (or none) are welcome.
Do I need to be a member of First UU San Diego?
No. You don't need to be a church member or have any affiliation with First UU San Diego.
Is there a cost to attend?
Fourth Monday meetings are free. No dues or membership fee. Renewal weekends have a modest cost — financial support may be available.
Can I attend on Zoom?
Yes. Most Fourth Monday meetings are available via Zoom. Contact a co-convener for the link.
What do I bring to my first meeting?
Just yourself. A dish for the potluck is appreciated but never expected for first-timers.
What are focus groups?
Small, ongoing groups of 5–8 men meeting regularly for deeper sharing and support. Interest begins at a Fourth Monday.

The Handbook — How We Be Together

Norms & Traditions

These are the agreements and practices that make the fellowship a safe and meaningful space. They are not rules — they are invitations.

01
Confidentiality

What is shared in the circle stays in the circle. Men speak freely because trust is the ground we stand on.

02
No Cross-Talk During Check-In

When a man is checking in, we listen without comment, advice, or response. He speaks; we receive. There is time for dialogue in the discussion that follows.

03
Speak from Your Own Experience

Use "I" statements. We share our own stories, not prescriptions for others. "I feel..." not "You should..."

04
Step Up, Step Back

If you tend toward silence, step into the circle more. If you tend to fill space, leave room for others. We take care of the whole group's voice.

05
Chants Are Sung Together

Chants are sung three times — the first time robust, the second louder, the third quieter or hummed. Tune out your inner critic and just sing.

06
The Potluck Is Part of the Gathering

Breaking bread together is the first act of fellowship. Come early enough to share the meal. First-timers are never expected to bring food.

Where We Came From

Our History

Roots

Our Home: First UU San Diego

The UUMF meets at First Unitarian Universalist Church of San Diego — one of the oldest congregations in the city. Unitarians first gathered here in 1873, when a small Sunday School met at Horton Hall on June 22 of that year. By 1877, the group had grown and formally organized as The Unitarian Society of San Diego.

The Hillcrest Campus — where the UUMF holds its Fourth Monday gatherings in the Common Room — was built in 1959. The church has been a center for liberal religion, free thought, and social justice in San Diego for over 150 years.

One of the church's founding members, Alonzo Erasmus Horton — the developer whose name still marks a neighborhood downtown — was among San Diego's early Unitarians.

The Mountains

Camp de Benneville Pines

The UUMF's Spring Renewal takes place at Camp de Benneville Pines, a UU camp in the San Bernardino National Forest above Angelus Oaks — at 6,800 feet elevation, surrounded by pine, cedar, and oak.

The camp was founded in 1961, purchased by UU congregations from San Diego to Santa Monica to Arizona pooling their resources. It serves over 60 groups a year and has been the setting for some of the most significant weekends in men's lives.

Broader Movement

The Southern California UU Men's Network

UUMF is one of several UU men's fellowships in Southern California. The Fall Renewal near Julian brings these groups together for a multi-congregation gathering — men from San Diego to other UU churches across the region spending three days in the mountains together.

What Has Endured

Whatever year the fellowship began, certain things have remained constant: a circle of men showing up for each other, month after month. A table with food on it. A check-in round where every voice is heard. Chants sung together. The mountains, twice a year.

The history is not only in the past. Every man who walks through the door for the first time is adding to it.
